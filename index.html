INSIDE THE WORK

What happens before
performance is tested.

Mental performance work becomes useful when it can survive the exact conditions that challenge an athlete. This is the practical layer between assessment, training and competition.

START HERE

Understand the performance environment.

The work begins with the sport, the athlete's role, the competition environment and the moments where performance tends to change under pressure.

What happensSport demands, role, routines, competition context and previous pressure points are mapped.
Athlete works onAwareness of patterns rather than trying to immediately fix them.
What gets measuredRelevant performance behaviours, attention patterns and pressure responses.
DIAGNOSE THE MOMENT

Find what changes when the stakes rise.

Attention, confidence, emotional regulation, decision-making, routines, communication and recovery can all behave differently under pressure.

What happensPerformance breakdowns are translated into specific, observable patterns.
Athlete works onRecognising the trigger → response sequence instead of treating pressure as one vague problem.
What gets measuredConsistency of response, decision quality and execution around pressure moments.
TRAIN

Turn insight into an executable response.

Mental skills are built around the demands of the sport—not as generic motivation, but as routines an athlete can actually use.

What happensSport-specific cues, breathing, visualisation, attentional strategies and routines are developed where appropriate.
Athlete works onMaking the response simple enough to execute during real performance.
What gets measuredUse, consistency and effectiveness of the selected performance behaviours.
APPLY

Practise the response before the competition asks for it.

The goal is not to create a calm bubble. It is to rehearse useful responses while expectations, consequences and pressure are present.

What happensPressure situations are recreated through realistic scenarios and sport-specific practice.
Athlete works onReturning to process, cue and decision rather than chasing a particular feeling.
What gets measuredResponse reliability when pressure is introduced.
COMPETE

Stop trying to feel confident. Execute the plan.

Competition becomes the test of whether the mental response is usable. The athlete returns to cues, routines, decisions and controllables.

What happensThe athlete applies the performance playbook in the actual competitive environment.
Athlete works onProcess focus, recovery after errors and staying connected to the next action.
What gets measuredTransfer from training into competition behaviour and execution.
ADAPT

Use the competition as information.

The cycle does not end when the event ends. What happened becomes evidence for the next adjustment to the athlete's performance playbook.

What happensPerformance is reviewed against the original pressure map and response plan.
Athlete works onKeeping what worked, changing what did not and sharpening the next response.
What gets measuredProgress over repeated performance cycles—not one isolated result.

The objective isn't to eliminate pressure.
It's to make performance more reliable when pressure arrives.
